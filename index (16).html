The Blizzard Menu at a Glance

The Blizzard menu is organised into four families, and understanding them makes choosing easy. Limited Time flavours arrive with the seasons and leave again, so they reward anyone who orders promptly. Signature Creations are the layered, recipe style builds, where several toppings are combined on purpose. Candy Classics pair famous candies with vanilla soft serve in the simplest, most nostalgic way. Royal Blizzards are the premium line, built around a filled centre of fudge, caramel or fruit that you discover halfway through the cup.

Every Blizzard starts the same way: DQ soft serve, famous for its smooth, dense texture, blended at the counter with your chosen mix-ins until the whole cup is one thick, spoonable treat. That thickness is not a marketing line. The Blizzard is traditionally served upside down to prove it, a ritual that began with the product itself in 1985 and still happens at counters today. If a blend ever failed that test, it would not be a Blizzard at all, and that simple standard explains why the texture feels different from an ordinary sundae or shake.

Prices on this page are careful US planning averages. Dairy Queen restaurants are locally owned, so the exact price at your store can be a little higher or lower, limited time flavours rotate, and extra mix-ins usually add a small upcharge. Treat every number here as a close guide, confirm the final total in the DQ app or at the counter, and you will never be surprised. Calories are listed the same honest way, as per size ranges to help you pick the portion that fits your day.

Blizzard Sizes and Prices, Explained

There are four standard sizes. The Mini, at about six ounces, is a genuine taste size, perfect after a meal or for younger guests. The Small, around twelve ounces, is the everyday solo treat. The Medium, around sixteen ounces, is the most shared size on the menu. The Large, around twenty one ounces, is a commitment or a two person dessert, and ordering it to share is usually smarter and cheaper than two smaller cups. Royal Blizzards and extra toppings typically add fifty cents to a dollar at each size.

Choosing a size is really choosing an occasion. A Mini after lunch keeps the afternoon light. A Small is the classic drive thru treat. A Medium rewards a proper dessert run, and a Large belongs at a table with two spoons and no hurry. Regulars decide the size before the flavour, because size controls both the price and the calories more than any single mix-in choice, and that one habit prevents most over ordering.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Dairy Queen Blizzard?

A Blizzard is DQ soft serve blended thick with candy, cookie, brownie or fruit mix-ins, traditionally served upside down to prove its thickness. It launched in 1985 and became the signature treat of Dairy Queen.

How much does a Blizzard cost?

As US planning averages, a Mini starts around $3.49 to $3.99, a Small around $4.49 to $4.99, a Medium around $5.29 to $5.89 and a Large around $6.19 to $6.89. Locally owned stores set final prices, and Royal flavours and extra mix-ins cost a little more.

Which Blizzard flavour is the most popular?

The Oreo Cookie Blizzard is the long running favourite for most guests, with Reese's Peanut Butter Cup and Chocolate Chip Cookie Dough close behind. Limited time flavours often outsell everything while they are available.

What sizes do Blizzards come in?

Four sizes: Mini (about 6 oz), Small (about 12 oz), Medium (about 16 oz) and Large (about 21 oz). Calories rise with size more than with flavour, so size is the first choice to make.

Can I add extra mix-ins to a Blizzard?

Yes. Extra candy, cookie or topping blends are a normal request for a small upcharge, usually around $0.50 to $0.99. Ask clearly at the counter or in the app.

Are Blizzard prices the same everywhere?

No. DQ restaurants are locally owned, so prices vary by store, city and state. Use this page for planning averages and confirm the final price in the app or at your location.

What is a Royal Blizzard?

A Royal Blizzard is a premium Blizzard with a filled centre of fudge, caramel or fruit sauce in the middle of the cup, such as the Royal New York Cheesecake or Royal Ultimate Choco Brownie.

Does Dairy Queen have seasonal Blizzard flavours?

Yes. Limited time flavours rotate through the year, such as Pumpkin Pie in fall and Cotton Candy in summer months. When a seasonal flavour you love appears, order it promptly because it will leave again.

The biscuit sandwiches lead the menu, especially Bacon, Egg and Cheese and Sausage, Egg and Cheese. Hash browns are the most added side, and the breakfast burritos are the top choice for eating on the move.

How much does breakfast at DQ cost?

Can I get breakfast items all day?

What comes in a breakfast combo?

Typically a biscuit sandwich, hash browns and a hot coffee for one bundled price that beats buying the three separately. Exact combo contents vary a little by location.

Is DQ breakfast good for a road trip?

What Makes a DQ Shake Different

A Dairy Queen milkshake starts from the same soft serve that made the brand famous, blended with milk and flavouring until it pours slowly and drinks thick. That soft serve base gives DQ shakes their particular smoothness: no icy crystals, no thin, milky finish, just a dense, creamy texture that holds a straw upright in the larger sizes. It is the oldest style of American shake making, and DQ has kept it because it works.

How much is a milkshake at Dairy Queen?

What milkshake flavours does DQ have?

The classic board includes vanilla, chocolate, strawberry, hot fudge, caramel, peanut butter, banana, cherry and mocha styles, plus cookie and seasonal flavours at many stores. This page covers sixteen of the most ordered.

What is the difference between a shake and a Blizzard?

A shake is blended completely smooth and drunk through a straw. A Blizzard keeps chunky mix-ins folded through thick soft serve and is eaten with a spoon, traditionally served upside down.

Can I order a shake extra thick?

Yes, extra thick is a normal request. The blend runs longer with a little less milk, giving a spoon worthy texture. Malts can also be made extra thick at most stores.

Can I mix two shake flavours?

Usually yes. Half and half orders such as chocolate banana or strawberry banana are everyday requests at most counters, and they are the easiest way to create a custom flavour.

Which DQ shake has the most calories?

Peanut butter and hot fudge styles sit at the rich end, around 640 to 720 calories for a regular. Vanilla is among the lightest classics. See the Nutrition page for the full picture by item.

Does DQ still make malts?

Many locations will finish a shake with malt flavouring on request, in the classic soda shop style. Availability varies by store, so ask at the counter.

Are milkshakes available all day?

Yes, shakes are part of the all day treat menu at DQ locations, unlike breakfast, which is morning only at participating stores.

How many calories are in a Blizzard?

Which Blizzard has the fewest calories?

How much sugar is in a DQ shake?

On Dairy Queen's official website and in store on request. Use this page for planning and comparison, and the official source for allergies, medical needs or exact current figures.

Does a Royal Blizzard have more calories than a regular Blizzard?

How can I make my usual order lighter?

Order one size down, ask for a lighter hand with toppings, skip extra mix-ins, share large sizes, and pair a rich treat with a lighter meal earlier in the day. Small changes beat giving up the treat entirely.

No. It is general planning information in plain language. For medical, allergy or dietary treatment questions, use official DQ information and your own clinician.

When was Dairy Queen founded?

Dairy Queen began in 1940 in Joliet, Illinois, where the first store sold soft serve for ten cents on its opening day. The brand grew town by town through locally owned stores.

When was the Blizzard invented?

The Blizzard Treat launched in 1985 and became an immediate phenomenon, famous for being blended so thick it is served upside down. It remains the signature product of the brand.

Why is a Blizzard served upside down?

To prove its thickness. The ritual demonstrates that a properly blended Blizzard holds together, and it has been part of the product's identity since launch.
